Assignment 1 · Modules 01–06

Regression Analysis for Pricing Prediction

Problem
How accurately can the market price of a product or property — used cars, laptops, and houses — be predicted from its features using regression models?
Method
Built a six-module pipeline with pandas and scikit-learn: data acquisition, wrangling with logged imputation decisions, EDA with correlation analysis, simple/multiple/polynomial regression, then cross-validation with Ridge regularisation tuned by GridSearchCV. Applied the same workflow to a 21,613-row King County house-price dataset.
Result
engine-size dominates automobile price (r = 0.872), while square footage dominates house price. A well-validated linear model beats more flexible ones: Ridge regression with a grid-searched alpha lifts the test-set R² compared with plain polynomial regression.
Deliverable
Six module notebooks, the datasets and figures they consume, and a README that reproduces the whole analysis. Everything lives in the project repository.
Limitation
Trained on 1985 automobile data and a 2014–2015 housing window; the workflow transfers to other markets, the absolute price levels do not.

02

Preliminary Project · pair work with Fong Hoi Lam (mc654953)

Is Macau's Transport Keeping Up With Its Visitors?

Problem
Macau draws millions of visitors into three very different zones. Is the public bus network carrying them where the demand actually is, or is capacity concentrated in the wrong place and the wrong hour?
Method
Assembled a zone-hour panel from visitor arrival records and bus departure records, computed each zone's share of visitor demand against its share of daily departures and stops, and expressed the mismatch as a supply-demand ratio. Profiled demand by hour of day and compared it with departure profiles to locate the timing gap.
Result
Capacity is uneven: the Peninsula is over-served with a supply-demand ratio of 1.577, Taipa & Cotai absorbs most visitor demand but few departures (0.507), and Coloane is thinly demanded but well covered (2.988). The timing mismatch mirrors this — visitor demand peaks at 16:00 while departures peak at 08:00.
Deliverable
Proposal document, verification notebook, two submitted figures, and the processed zone-level and hourly matching tables, all in the project repository.
Limitation
Uses bus departures as the supply proxy; private cars, taxis, light rail and walking are outside scope, and the analysis covers a limited data window.

Approach

How I work through a dataset.

The same four moves run through every case above. What changes is the data; what stays fixed is the order of the decisions and the fact that each one leaves a trace.

Step 01

Acquire and profile

Load the raw file as it is, restore meaning (headers, units, placeholders), then look before touching: types, counts, distributions, cardinality. Nothing is cleaned until the shape of the mess is known.

Step 02

Clean with logged decisions

Every imputation, conversion and drop is a judgement, not a chore. Missing values are filled only where the field is genuinely recoverable; rows are dropped only when the target is absent; conversions keep units visible in the column name.

Step 03

Model, then test honestly

Start with the simplest model that can answer the question. Measure against held-out data, not the training set. When a flexible model such as a polynomial loses to multiple linear regression, the flexible model is the one that goes.

Step 04

Communicate the evidence

Charts are chosen to answer a question: a scatter for a relationship, a box plot for a distribution, a heatmap for a matrix, a ratio bar for a mismatch. The conclusion has to be visible without reading the code.

Raw data→Profile→Impute & encode→Correlation & EDA→Regression→Cross-validation & regularisation→Recommendation
